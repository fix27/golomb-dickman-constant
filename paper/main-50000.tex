\begin{abstract}
We describe a rigorous interval-arithmetic certificate for the first
50\,000 decimal digits of the Golomb--Dickman constant
\[
  \lam=\int_0^\infty e^{-u-\Eone(u)}\,du
      =0.624329988543550870992936383100837244\ldots,
\]
where $\Eone$ is the exponential integral. The certificate consists of
integers $\Lambda_-<\Lambda_+$ and a scale $S=166\,293$ such that
$\lam\in[\Lambda_-/2^S,\Lambda_+/2^S]$ and
\[
  \floor{10^{50000}\Lambda_-/2^S}
  =\floor{10^{50000}\Lambda_+/2^S}.
\]
Consequently the digits are \emph{proved by truncation}, not merely
computed in floating point. The interval width is
$4.390\times10^{-50043}$, which leaves about $42.36$ guard digits. The
computation evaluates
$\lam=\int_{\R}G(t)\,dt$ by the trapezoidal rule after the
Takahashi--Mori substitution, with explicit tail and discretisation
bounds in the spirit of Trefethen--Weideman and with every node enclosed
in an Arb ball. We give the error budget, the certificate format, a
checker that uses only exact integer arithmetic, and a b-file for OEIS
A084945 with terms $n=0,\ldots,49999$. We also report measurements on the
resulting digit string, extend the published OEIS A225242 bound
$a(5)>15\,000$ and our earlier bounds $a(5),a(6)>40\,000$ to
$a(5),a(6)>50\,000$, and audit the statements of the MathWorld page.
The trust base is the correctness of Arb together with the cited
theorems; this is not a formalisation in a proof assistant. We make no
claim of a world record and no claim about the normality of $\lam$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

For $n\ge2$ let $P(n)$ be the largest prime factor of $n$. The
Golomb--Dickman constant is the limit of the average of
$\log P(n)/\log n$,
\begin{equation}
  \lam=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\sum_{k=2}^n
       \frac{\log P(k)}{\log k}.
  \label{eq:mean}
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
  \lam=\int_0^\infty F(u)\,du,\qquad F(u)=e^{-u-\Eone(u)} .
  \label{eq:lambda}
\end{equation}
Dickman's function $\rho(u)$ gives the limiting density of integers whose
largest prime factor is at most $n^{1/u}$ \cite{dickman1930}; the constant
in \eqref{eq:mean} is the mean of $\log P(n)/\log n$, not the mean of
$P(n)/n$. The asymptotic analysis of $\rho$ is classical
\cite{debruijn1951}.

Numerical values are collected in OEIS A084945 \cite{oeisA084945} and on
the MathWorld page \emph{Golomb--Dickman Constant Digits}
\cite{mathworld}. When this work began, the OEIS b-file contained the
terms $n=0,\ldots,101$ (102 terms), synthesised from the entry itself.
MathWorld reports $15\,000$ digits, attributed to Weisstein (2013); we
treat that attribution as historical and have marked it
\texttt{NOT\_VERIFIABLE} in our audit, while every checkable statement
about those digits is consistent with the certified string. The present computation extends
the verified decimal expansion to $50\,000$ digits and supplies a
certificate that makes the verification independent of any particular
floating-point implementation. A $40\,000$-digit certificate was
assembled earlier; the present paper reports the $50\,000$-digit
extension and its audit.

The contribution is deliberately narrow. We do not claim a new
algorithm: the method is the exponentially convergent trapezoidal rule
\cite{TW2014} applied after a substitution that produces double
exponential decay at both ends, with the exponential integral evaluated
by a power series, a Stieltjes continued fraction \cite{stieltjes1894},
or an asymptotic expansion, whichever is cheapest at the node. The
contribution is (a) a complete error budget at $D=50\,000$ digits, (b) an
integer certificate whose verification needs only exact arithmetic, and
(c) a reproducible artefact set, including checkpoints and a
dependency-free verifier. This is an extension of the certified
expansion, not a world-record claim.

\paragraph{Notation.}
$\Eone(u)=\int_u^\infty e^{-s}s^{-1}ds$; $\gamma$ is Euler's constant;
$D$ is the number of certified decimal digits, $G$ the number of guard
digits, and $S$ the binary scale of the certificate.

\section{The integral and the substitution}

Set
\begin{equation}
  F(u)=e^{-u-\Eone(u)},\qquad \lam=\int_0^\infty F(u)\,du .
\end{equation}
For $u>0$ one has $0<F(u)<\min(e^\gamma u,\,e^{-u})$, and $F$ extends to
an entire function of $u$ through
\begin{equation}
  F(u)=e^{\gamma}u\exp\!\Bigl(-2u+\sum_{k\ge2}\frac{(-u)^k}{k\,k!}\Bigr),
  \label{eq:Fseries}
\end{equation}
which follows from the classical expansion of $\Eone$
\cite[5.1.11]{AS1964}.

The original integrand has a logarithmic singularity at $u=0$: it
vanishes like $u$ on the left and decays like $e^{-u}$ on the right, so
the trapezoidal rule on $(0,\infty)$ converges only algebraically. The
Takahashi--Mori substitution
\begin{equation}
  u=u(t)=\exp\bigl(t-e^{-t}\bigr),\qquad t\in\R,
  \label{eq:subst}
\end{equation}
is an increasing bijection $\R\to(0,\infty)$ with
$du=u(t)(1+e^{-t})\,dt$. Hence
\begin{equation}
  \lam=\int_{\R}G(t)\,dt,\qquad
  G(t)=F(u(t))\,u(t)\,(1+e^{-t}),
  \label{eq:G}
\end{equation}
and $G$ is entire in $t$ and decays double exponentially at both ends:
there is an absolute constant $c>0$ with
\begin{equation}
  G(t)=O\bigl(e^{-c\,e^{|t|}}\bigr).
  \label{eq:decay}
\end{equation}
In particular, the substitution turns the algebraic behaviour at $u=0$
into double exponential decay; the double exponential decay is produced
by the substitution, not removed by it.

Two elementary tail identities are used below. For every $t_0$,
\begin{equation}
  \int_{t_0}^{\infty}u\,e^{-u}(1+e^{-t})\,dt=e^{-u(t_0)},\qquad
  \int_{-\infty}^{t_0}e^{\gamma}u^2(1+e^{-t})\,dt
  =\tfrac12 e^{\gamma}u(t_0)^2 .
  \label{eq:tails}
\end{equation}
They follow by the substitution $u=u(t)$ and the bounds of
\eqref{eq:Fseries}.

\section{Quadrature with explicit bounds}

Fix a step $h>0$ and integers $K_L,K_R$ with $u(K_Rh)\ge1$. Write
\begin{equation}
  Q_h=h\sum_{k=-K_L}^{K_R}G(kh).
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Truncation]
\label{lem:tail}
With $G$ as in \eqref{eq:G},
\begin{equation}
  0\le h\!\!\sum_{k>K_R}\!G(kh)\le e^{-u(K_Rh)},\qquad
  0\le h\!\!\sum_{k<-K_L}\!G(kh)\le \tfrac12 e^{\gamma}u(-K_Lh)^2 .
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:Fseries}, $0<G\le g_R:=ue^{-u}(1+e^{-t})$ and
$0<G\le g_L:=e^\gamma u^2(1+e^{-t})$. The function $g_R$ decreases for
$u\ge1$, so $hg_R(kh)\le\int_{(k-1)h}^{kh}g_R$ and the right tail is at
most $\int_{K_Rh}^\infty g_R=e^{-u(K_Rh)}$ by \eqref{eq:tails}. For
$g_L$ one has $\frac{d}{dt}\log g_L=2(1+e^{-t})-e^{-t}/(1+e^{-t})>0$, so
$hg_L(kh)\le\int_{kh}^{(k+1)h}g_L$ and the left tail is at most
$\frac12e^\gamma u(-K_Lh)^2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Discretisation]
\label{lem:disc}
Let $d=7/5$, $c=\cos d>0$, and suppose $G$ is analytic in the strip
$|\operatorname{Im}z|<d$ with
$\int_\R|G(x+iy)|\,dx\le M$ there. Then
\begin{equation}
  \Bigl|\lam-Q_h\Bigr|
  \le B_{\mathrm{disc}}+\tau_R+\tau_L,
  \qquad
  B_{\mathrm{disc}}=\frac{2M}{e^{2\pi d/h}-1},
\end{equation}
where $\tau_R,\tau_L$ are the tail bounds of Lemma~\ref{lem:tail}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The quadrature error is bounded by the Trefethen--Weideman trapezoidal
rule estimate \cite[Thm.~5.1]{TW2014}, and the two tails by
Lemma~\ref{lem:tail}. The decay required in the strip follows from the
estimates below.
\end{proof}

For $G$ one may take
\begin{equation}
  M=M_1+M_2+M_3,
  \label{eq:M}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{align}
  M_1&=C_1e^{-2c}\Bigl(\frac1c+\frac1{4c^2}\Bigr),
  &C_1&=e^{\gamma+2+(e-2)/2},\\
  M_2&=4e^{\gamma}e^{4}\exp\!\Bigl(2e^2+\tfrac12e^{e^2}\Bigr),\\
  M_3&=\frac{e^{\,e^{e^{-2}-2}}\,(1+e^{-2})\,e^{-\kappa'e^2}}{\kappa'},
  &\kappa'&=\cos(\theta_{\max})\,e^{-e^{-2}},
\end{align}
and $\theta_{\max}=d+e^{-2}\sin d<\pi/2$. The three terms bound the
strips $t\le0$, $0\le t\le2$ and $t\ge2$ respectively. The middle term
is deliberately crude and gives $M\approx10^{360}$; this is harmless,
because the discretisation error at
$h=80\,846\,368/2^{40}\approx7.35\times10^{-5}$ is smaller than
$10^{-51594}$.

\section{Enclosing the nodes}

Every value $G(kh)$ is enclosed in an Arb ball \cite{johansson2017}, and
the radius of that ball is checked to be below $2^{-E_{\mathrm{bits}}}$;
the check is part of the postcondition of the node routine, not an
heuristic. Arb provides midpoint--radius interval arithmetic with
directed rounding, so each elementary operation widens the enclosure in
a certified way.

Three independent representations of $\Eone$ are used, and the routine
chooses the cheapest one that is guaranteed to reach the target radius.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
\item \emph{Power series.} From \eqref{eq:Fseries} with
  $a_j=(-u)^j/(j\,j!)$, the ratio test gives
  $|a_{j+1}/a_j|=uj/(j+1)^2\le u/(j+1)$. If $K+1\ge2u$, the tail is at
  most $2|a_{K+1}|$; the finite part is summed in Arb and the tail is
  added as a symmetric interval.
\item \emph{Stieltjes continued fraction.} For $z>0$,
  \begin{equation}
    e^z\Eone(z)=\frac1z\,g(1/z),\qquad
    g(x)=\int_0^\infty\frac{e^{-s}}{1+sx}\,ds .
  \end{equation}
  The measure $e^{-s}ds$ has moments $m_j=j!$, and Carleman's condition
  holds, so the Stieltjes transform is determined by its moments. The
  J-fraction obtained from the Laguerre recurrence compresses to the
  S-fraction with coefficients $\alpha_{2j-1}=\alpha_{2j}=j>0$. For a
  positive Stieltjes fraction, consecutive convergents bracket the
  value. If $f_n$ denotes the $n$-th convergent to $e^z\Eone(z)$ and
  $D_n$ bounds $|f_n-f_{n-1}|$ in interval arithmetic, then
  $f_n+[-D_n,D_n]$ contains $e^z\Eone(z)$ and hence the node ball.
\item \emph{Asymptotic expansion.} From
  $e^u\Eone(u)=\int_0^\infty e^{-s}/(u+s)\,ds$ and the finite geometric
  identity,
  \begin{equation}
    e^u\Eone(u)=\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}\frac{(-1)^j j!}{u^{j+1}}+R_K,
    \qquad |R_K|\le\frac{K!}{u^{K+1}} .
  \end{equation}
  This is used only where $u$ is large enough for the bound to be below
  the target radius.
\end{enumerate}

A fourth branch handles nodes whose value is provably negligible: from
$0<F(u)<\min(e^\gamma u,e^{-u})$ one gets
$0<G(t)\le e^\gamma u^2(1+e^{-t})$ for $t<0$ and
$0<G(t)\le ue^{-u}(1+e^{-t})$ for $t\ge0$. The branch returns $[0,U]$
only after Arb has certified $U<\varepsilon$; otherwise the regular
branch is used.

\begin{remark}
The choice of branch, the number of series terms and the number of
continued-fraction levels are made using floating-point estimates.
Those estimates only select resources. The enclosure and the radius
check are always performed in Arb; if the requested accuracy is not
reached, the precision is raised and the node is recomputed, or the
program aborts. A node is never accepted on the strength of a
floating-point estimate.
\end{remark}

\section{The certificate and its error budget}

Let $S=E_{\mathrm{bits}}+64$. Each accepted node contributes an interval
$[g_k^-,g_k^+]$ with $G(kh)\in[g_k^-,g_k^+]$ and
$\rad<2^{-E_{\mathrm{bits}}}$, expressed in integer units of $2^{-S}$.
The computation stores, for every chunk of nodes, the exact integer sums
of the lower and upper endpoints. Summing the chunks gives integers
$L_{\mathrm{sum}}$ and $H_{\mathrm{sum}}$ with
\begin{equation}
  h\,2^{-S}L_{\mathrm{sum}}\le Q_h\le h\,2^{-S}H_{\mathrm{sum}} .
\end{equation}
The certificate then records
\begin{equation}
  \Lambda_-=\floor{hL_{\mathrm{sum}}}-B,\qquad
  \Lambda_+=\lceil hH_{\mathrm{sum}}\rceil+C,\qquad
  C=T_R+T_L+B,
  \label{eq:cert}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
  B=\floor{B_{\mathrm{disc}}\,2^S}+1,\qquad
  T_R=\floor{\tau_R\,2^S}+1,\qquad
  T_L=\floor{\tau_L\,2^S}+1 .
\end{equation}
Thus every quantity that enters $\Lambda_\pm$ is either an exact integer
or an outward-rounded bound; no fitted constant is involved.

resulting bounds. The corrections $B$ and $C$ are reproduced
independently by \texttt{verification/audit\_chunks.py} from the chunk
sums; the individual tail corrections follow from the formulas above.

The discretisation bound is about $1595$ orders of magnitude below what
is needed, and each tail is at least $43$ orders below the target
$10^{-50000}$. The width is dominated by the accumulated widths of the

$4.230\times10^{-50043}$ ($2^{55.83}$ units of $2^{-S}$), while the

correction $C=2.4277\times10^{15}\approx2^{51.1}$ units of $2^{-S}$ adds

control the guard-digit count; the interval arithmetic does. The first
$40\,000$ digits of the new certificate agree with the earlier

The certificate is a four-line text file: a comment line naming the
constant and the method, a comment line with $S$ and $\Lambda_-$, a
comment line with $\Lambda_+$, and a line containing
$0.d_1d_2\cdots d_D$. Verification needs only exact integer arithmetic.

\section{Verification}

The verification consists of the following exact checks.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
\item Parse $S,\Lambda_-,\Lambda_+$ and the digit string.
\item Check $0<\Lambda_-<\Lambda_+$ and that $\Lambda_-/2^S$ is on the
  expected scale.
\item Check $\floor{10^D\Lambda_-/2^S}=\floor{10^D\Lambda_+/2^S}$;
  this is the statement that the interval does not cross a decimal cell.
\item Check that the common value, written with $D$ leading zeros if
  necessary, equals the digit string.
\item Check that the standalone digits file equals the certificate line.
\item Check that the b-file has exactly $D$ terms, indexed
  $0,\ldots,D-1$, each equal to the corresponding digit, in plain ASCII
  with LF line endings.
\item Check that the first 102 digits equal the published prefix of OEIS
  A084945; optionally, with \texttt{--compare}, that the first
  $40\,000$ digits equal the earlier certified string.
\end{enumerate}

Steps 1--7 are implemented in \texttt{verification/verify\_certificate.py}
with the Python standard library only, and run in about $0.14$\,s. In the
published run the optional comparison agreed for all $40\,000$ leading
digits. The verifier also computes and prints the SHA-256 digest of each
input; digests can be pinned with \texttt{--sha256}, and the canonical
values are listed in Appendix~\ref{app:sums}. The repository contains a
secondary audit that re-reads all $3\,076$ checkpoints, recomputes the
exact integer sums, checks the covered node range and the planned step,
re-derives $B$ and $C$, and compares the chunk-sum width with the
certificate width (\texttt{verification/audit\_chunks.py}). In the
published run this audit reports $3\,076$ chunks covering $307\,580$
nodes, no gaps, planned step $80\,846\,368/2^{40}$, $B=1$,
$C=2\,427\,712\,667\,995\,762$, chunk-sum width
$4.230\times10^{-50043}$, and certificate width
$4.390\times10^{-50043}$. The audit treats the saved intervals as a
cache: it re-derives the integer sums from them but does not recompute
the Arb balls. As a control, the same audit applied to the
$40\,000$-digit run, whose plan is stored in the older \texttt{meta.pkl}
format, recovers $m=100\,859\,575$ and $C=65\,673\,984\,721\,697$,
exactly the documented values.

The repository organises the checks in three levels, so that a reader
can pay exactly as much as the question they care about costs
(\texttt{verification/}). Level~A verifies the certificate and the digit
strings with exact integer arithmetic and no external dependency
(\texttt{verify\_certificate.py}). Level~B audits all chunks from the
saved intervals, re-derives the integer corrections and checks the
recorded plan (\texttt{audit\_chunks.py}); the
$40\,000$-digit package additionally contains
\texttt{checks/verify\_nodes\_decimal.py}, whose \texttt{--plan} mode
reproduces the analytic bounds and corrections of the $40\,000$-digit
run, and \texttt{checks/verify\_checkpoints\_arb.py}, which re-implements
the three representations of $\Eone$ and can recompute all nodes in Arb.
Level~C recomputes the leading digits with a different representation of
the integral, a different quadrature rule and a different library
(\texttt{independent\_quadrature.py}, \texttt{mpmath}). Level~C is
floating point, hence numerical evidence rather than a proof; at present
it covers $1\,000$ digits, for which the two quadrature rules agree with
each other and with the certificate for the first $1\,020$ digits. It
does not cover $50\,000$ digits. Likewise, the twelve-chunk
recomputation in \texttt{Decimal} that accompanies the $40\,000$-digit
package was not repeated at $50\,000$ digits; the $50\,000$-digit
Level~B evidence is the chunk-sum audit described above.

The Arb re-audit is the only check that re-does the Arb evaluation
independently of the producing program. It was not executed on the
machine used to assemble this package, because an Application Control
policy blocked the Arb shared libraries; it is included for a host
without that restriction. No analogous full Arb re-audit was run for the
$50\,000$-digit run. Consequently the certificate presented here is
conditional on the producing program having computed every ball
correctly, on Arb being correct, and on the elementary lemmas of
Sections~3 and~4. It is not a formally verified proof.

and the page's statements; they are not a proof of normality and not a
world-record claim.

\section{Reproducibility and data}

The producing pipeline is \texttt{golomb\_dickman\_26.py} in the
\texttt{50000/} directory. It requires Python 3.11+ and
\texttt{python-flint} \cite{pythonflint} (Arb); the run takes hours of CPU
and is resumable through checkpoints. The plan is recorded in
\texttt{gd\_rigorous\_ckpt\_50000/meta\_v12.json}; it fixes
$h=80\,846\,368/2^{40}$, $K_L=149\,075$ and $K_R=158\,504$. The
repository ships the $3\,076$ published chunks, so the certificate can be
re-derived without recomputing the nodes:

\begin{lstlisting}
python -m pip install python-flint
cd 50000
python ../verification/verify_certificate.py \
    --cert golomb_dickman_50000_certified.txt \
    --compare ../40000/golomb_dickman_40000_digits.txt
python ../verification/audit_chunks.py \
    --ckpt gd_rigorous_ckpt_50000 \
    --cert golomb_dickman_50000_certified.txt
python golomb_dickman_26.py certify --digits 50000   # hours
\end{lstlisting}

The current self-contained command-line interface accepts
\texttt{certify --digits 50000}, \texttt{crosscheck},
\texttt{boundary-test}, \texttt{test} and \texttt{smoke}.

\paragraph{Data.}
The certificate \texttt{golomb\_dickman\_50000\_certified.txt}, the
digit file \texttt{golomb\_dickman\_50000\_digits.txt}, the OEIS b-file
\texttt{b084945.txt}, the code, the verifiers and the checkpoints are
archived in the GitHub repository
\url{https://github.com/fix27/golomb-dickman-constant/tree/main/50000}.
SHA-256 checksums of the canonical files are given in
Appendix~\ref{app:sums}. An archived release with a DOI is planned; the
current bundle is documented in \texttt{50000/README.md}.

\paragraph{Trust base.}
The result is conditional on:
(i) the correctness of Arb's midpoint--radius arithmetic
\cite{johansson2017};
(ii) the trapezoidal rule theorem of Trefethen and Weideman
\cite[Thm.~5.1]{TW2014};
(iii) the classical Stieltjes theorem on two-sided bounds by consecutive
convergents \cite{stieltjes1894};
(iv) the elementary lemmas of Sections~2--4, stated with proofs in the
docstring of \texttt{golomb\_dickman\_26.py} and in the $40\,000$-digit
verification package;
(v) exact integer arithmetic in Python.
No step depends on the normality of $\lam$, on unproved conjectures, or
on the numerical value of $\gamma$ beyond its certified enclosure.

\section{Conclusion}

The first $50\,000$ decimal digits of the Golomb--Dickman constant are
certified by an interval of width $4.390\times10^{-50043}$ whose
endpoints give the same $50\,000$-digit truncation. The certificate is
small, the verifier is dependency-free, and the b-file for OEIS A084945
extends the published data from $102$ terms ($n=0,\ldots,101$) to
$50\,000$ terms ($n=0,\ldots,49999$). The main open task is the
independent Arb re-audit of all chunks on an unrestricted host; the
$40\,000$-digit script is part of the package, and the $50\,000$-digit
run would use the same method. No world record and no statement about
the normality of $\lam$ is claimed.
